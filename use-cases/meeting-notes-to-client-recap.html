Turn meeting notes into a client recap email in 5 minutes

Short answer: Jot down decisions, action items with an owner and date, and open questions during the meeting. Afterward, paste the notes into an AI assistant using initials instead of client names, check every date and commitment, put the names back, and send the recap within the hour. Ask for consent before recording any call.

Setup time
about 10 minutes (estimate)
Skill level
Beginner
Cost
$0. Typed notes work; Fathom's free plan is optional.
Best for
Anyone who owes clients a written follow-up after meetings

Updated Oct 18, 2026 · By the Main Street AI Brief team · Facts checked against the sources below.

Send a clear recap of decisions, next steps and owners within the hour after every client meeting, drafted by AI from your notes and checked by you. Who it's for: Accountants, bookkeepers, agencies, consultants, insurance agents and law-office staff who meet with clients and owe them a written follow-up. Saves: 10 to 20 minutes per meeting (rough estimate)

The situation today

  • The meeting ends, the next one starts, and the recap gets written days later from memory, if at all.
  • Without a written recap, clients remember a different version of who was doing what by when.
  • Rough estimate: 15 to 30 minutes per recap when written from scratch.

Time figures marked "rough estimate" are our estimates, not survey data.

The AI-assisted workflow

Step by step

  1. During the meeting, jot down three things: decisions, action items with a name and date, and open questions. Fragments are fine.
  2. Right after, paste your notes into the prompt below. Use initials or roles instead of client names and account numbers.
  3. Check every date, amount and commitment against your notes. Delete anything the AI added.
  4. Put the names back in, then send it within the hour while the meeting is fresh.
  5. Save the recap in the client's file. It's your record of what was agreed.

Copy-paste prompt

Turn my meeting notes into a short recap email to a client.
Context: I'm a [accountant/consultant/agency owner]. Meeting with [client role, no names] on [date] about [topic].
My notes:
[paste notes]

Format:
- One-line thank-you
- 'What we decided' (bullets)
- 'Next steps': who, what, by when (table)
- 'Open questions' (bullets)
- One closing line with the next meeting or check-in date, if in my notes
Rules: use only what's in my notes. Don't add commitments, dates or advice. If an owner or date is missing, write [CHECK]. Under 200 words. Plain, warm, professional.

Works in any AI chat assistant. Replace everything in [brackets].

Example output (illustrative, not real data)

What we decided: move payroll to twice monthly starting next quarter; keep the current bookkeeping scope.

WhoWhatBy when
ClientSend last 3 months of bank statementsFriday
UsDraft revised engagement letterNext Wednesday
UsSet up payroll calendar[CHECK]

Open questions: Does the part-time hire start before or after the switch?

Price: Free forever for individuals, per Fathom's pricing page. Paid plans are also listed. (checked 2026-10-18 on the vendor's pricing page; prices change)

Skip it if: your meetings are mostly in person or by phone, your clients won't agree to being recorded, or your firm's data rules don't allow call recordings with an outside service. Typed notes plus the prompt above work fine.

Not sponsored. No affiliate link.

Watch-outs

  • Recording needs consent. Some states, including California, Florida, Illinois and Pennsylvania, generally require everyone on a call to agree to recording. Ask at the start, every time, and respect a no.
  • Don't paste client names, tax ID or Social Security numbers, account numbers, or health details into a general AI assistant. Use roles and initials.
  • Before using any recording or AI tool with client data, check where the data is stored and who can see it; professional rules for your field may apply.

When not to use AI

  • Recaps that contain advice (tax, legal, insurance coverage). Write the advice yourself; AI can format around it, not create it.
  • Meetings you're legally required to document in a specific way. Follow your profession's rules.

Educational content, not legal, tax or financial advice.

Quick answers

What should a client meeting recap include?

What was decided, the next steps with an owner and a date for each, any open questions, and when you'll talk next.

Is it legal to record client calls with an AI notetaker?

It depends on where you and your client are. Some states require everyone on a call to consent. Always ask at the start of the call, and check with your attorney if you record regularly.
