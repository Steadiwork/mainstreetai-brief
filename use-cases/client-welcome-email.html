How to write a client welcome email (with template)

Short answer: Write down what happens in a new client's first 30 days, what you need from them and how to reach you. Paste those notes into an AI chat with the prompt below and ask for a short email using only your facts. Check every date and promise, save it as a template, and send it the day they sign.

Setup time
about 20 minutes, once (estimate)
Skill level
Beginner
Cost
$0 with a free AI chat assistant.
Best for
Accountants, bookkeepers, agencies and consultants signing new clients

Updated Nov 9, 2026 · By the Main Street AI Brief team · Facts checked against the sources below.

Write one client welcome email with AI: what happens next, what you need from them, how to send documents and how to reach you. Free prompt and example. Who it's for: Accountants, bookkeepers, consultants, agencies and insurance offices who sign new clients and want every one to get the same clear first email. Saves: retyping the same email for each new client (rough estimate)

The situation today

  • A new client signs, then waits. They aren't sure what you need first, so they email to ask, or they do nothing.
  • Each welcome email gets written from scratch, so some clients hear about the document portal and some don't.
  • Rough estimate: writing and checking one reusable welcome email takes about 20 minutes, once.

Time figures marked "rough estimate" are our estimates, not survey data.

The AI-assisted workflow

Step by step

  1. List what really happens in a new client's first 30 days: kickoff meeting, first deliverable, any review call. Use dates relative to signing ("within a week").
  2. List what you need from the client and by when, and the one secure way they should send documents.
  3. Add who their contact is and how fast you reply. If you use AI in your work, add one honest line on how a person checks it.
  4. Paste your notes into the prompt below. Describe the client type, not the client, and leave out names and account details.
  5. Read the draft against your notes. Delete any service, date, fee or guarantee you didn't write.
  6. Save it as a template. For each new client, change the first line and the dates, then send it the day they sign.

Copy-paste prompt

I run a small [accounting / bookkeeping / agency / consulting / insurance] firm. Write a welcome email for a new [client type, e.g. "monthly bookkeeping client"]. Use only the facts in my notes. Don't invent dates, fees, guarantees, services or software. If something I need is missing, write [CHECK] instead of guessing.
My notes:
- What happens in the first 30 days: [paste]
- What I need from the client, and by when: [list]
- How to send documents securely: [portal or upload link]
- Who their contact is and how fast we reply: [details]
- How we use AI and who checks the work (optional): [one line]

Write: 1) A two-sentence warm welcome. 2) What happens next, as 3-5 dated steps. 3) What we need from you, as a short checklist. 4) How to reach us and when to expect a reply. 5) One line asking them to reply with any questions. Plain English, under 250 words, friendly but not gushing, no buzzwords.

Works in any AI chat assistant. Replace everything in [brackets].

Optional tool

A free AI chat assistant is enough for this job; save the final email as a template in the email or practice software you already use.

Watch-outs

  • Don't paste client names, tax IDs, account numbers or documents into a general AI assistant. The prompt only needs your process.
  • Point clients to a secure portal or upload link for documents. The IRS's Publication 4557 tells tax professionals to encrypt all sensitive files and emails, especially those with a taxpayer's personally identifiable information.
  • A welcome email isn't an engagement letter. Keep scope, fees and liability in your signed agreement, and check that wording with your attorney or your professional body's template.
  • AI can add a promise you never made, like a turnaround time or an extra service. Check each line before it becomes your template.

When not to use AI

  • Writing the engagement letter or contract terms themselves. Use your attorney's or professional body's template.
  • Clients in a sensitive situation, such as a tax notice or a dispute. Write that first email yourself.

Quick answers

What should a client welcome email include?

A short welcome, what happens in the first few weeks, what you need from the client and by when, how to send documents securely, and how to reach you and when to expect a reply.

Can ChatGPT write a client onboarding email?

It can draft one from your own notes if you tell it to use only your facts. Leave out client names and account details, and check every date and promise before you save it as a template.
