How to write a catering quote clients say yes to

Short answer: Write your catering basics once: price per person or per tray, delivery fee, deposit, final-count deadline and change terms. Paste the customer's request into an AI chat with those facts and ask for the total first, then a line-item table and three next steps. Check every number, then send it from your own email.

Turn a catering inquiry into a clear quote with line items, a deposit and a final-count deadline. Free AI prompt and fictional example. Who it's for: Owners of cafes, bakeries, restaurants and food trucks who get emails asking to cater office lunches, parties and events. Saves: the back-and-forth emails per inquiry (rough estimate)

The situation today

  • Catering inquiries often arrive as one line: a date, a headcount and "how much?" The reply that gets a yes is the one that shows a total, what it covers and what happens next.
  • DoorDash expanded DoorDash Catering to consumers and workplaces across the U.S. on Nov 2 and added an AI assistant that recommends a menu and portion sizes from group size, budget and dietary needs. Customers may be comparing your reply with that.
  • Rough estimate: a clear first quote can save several rounds of questions about deposits, headcount and delivery.

Time figures marked "rough estimate" are our estimates, not survey data.

The AI-assisted workflow

Step by step

  1. Write your catering basics once: price per person or per tray, minimum order if you have one, delivery or setup fee, deposit, and the number of days before the event you need the final guest count.
  2. Add your change and cancellation terms in one or two plain sentences, plus the allergens you can actually confirm for each menu item.
  3. Paste the customer's request into the prompt below: date, headcount, delivery or pickup and any dietary needs. Leave out names, phone numbers and addresses.
  4. Ask for the total first, then a line-item table, then three next steps: approve, pay the deposit, confirm the final count by a date.
  5. Check every price, date and allergen line against your own list. Delete anything the AI added that you didn't give it.
  6. Send it from your own email, and save the final version as your template for the next inquiry.

Copy-paste prompt

I run a [type of food business] in [town]. A customer asked for catering. Turn the inquiry and my facts below into a quote email they can say yes to.
Use only my facts. Don't invent prices, menu items, fees, availability or policies. If something is missing, write [CHECK].
Customer inquiry (no personal details): [paste what they asked for: date, guest count, delivery or pickup, dietary needs]
My menu and prices: [items and price per person or per tray]
Delivery or setup fee: [amount or none]
Deposit: [amount or percent, and due date]
Final guest count due: [days before the event]
Change and cancellation terms: [what I do]
Allergens I can confirm: [list, or 'ask me']

Write: 1) a reply under 200 words that leads with the total and what it covers; 2) a line-item table (Item | Qty | Price); 3) the three next steps: approve, pay the deposit, confirm the final count by [date]; 4) a one-line allergen note. Plain, friendly English. Then list the questions I still need to answer before sending.

Works in any AI chat assistant. Replace everything in [brackets].

Watch-outs

  • Keep customer details out of AI chats. Paste the date, headcount and food requests, never names, phone numbers, addresses or payment details.
  • Check every number. AI can invent a price, a fee or a deadline you never gave it. If it isn't in your facts, delete it.
  • Allergens: the FDA lists nine major food allergens: milk, eggs, fish, crustacean shellfish, tree nuts, peanuts, wheat, soybeans and sesame. Only state what your recipes and supplier labels confirm, and ask customers to tell you about allergies before the event. State and local food rules also apply; check yours.
  • Deposit and cancellation terms are your business decision. For a written contract or large events, have an attorney or your insurance agent check your wording.

When not to use AI

  • Custom weddings or large events that need a tasting, a site visit or a contract. Use the quote only as a starting point.
  • Pricing a job you haven't costed. Work out your food cost, labor and delivery first, then let AI do the writing.

Quick answers

What should a catering quote include?

A total price, what it covers (items and quantities), any delivery or setup fee, the deposit and when it's due, the deadline for the final guest count, and your change and cancellation terms. The prompt on this page asks for each of those.

Can I use AI to write a catering quote?

Yes, for the wording. Give it your own prices and terms, tell it not to invent anything, and check every number before you send.
