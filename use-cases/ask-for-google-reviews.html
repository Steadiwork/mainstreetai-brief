Ask customers for Google reviews: text and email scripts

Short answer: Copy your review link from your Business Profile (Read reviews, then Get more reviews), then have AI draft a short text and email that ask for an honest review. Send the same ask to every customer soon after the visit. Google bans offering anything in return and asking only happy customers.

Setup time
about 15 minutes, once (estimate)
Skill level
Beginner
Cost
$0. The review link and QR code come free from Google Business Profile.
Best for
Any owner with a Google Business Profile who wants more reviews

Updated Oct 26, 2026 · By the Main Street AI Brief team · Facts checked against the sources below.

A copy-paste prompt for a polite text and email asking customers for an honest Google review, plus where to get your review link and Google's rules. Who it's for: Any owner with a verified Google Business Profile: shops, restaurants, contractors, salons and offices. Works whether you see customers at a counter, at their home or by email. Saves: the rewriting each time you ask (rough estimate)

The situation today

  • Happy customers rarely think to leave a review unless someone asks, and asking in person can feel awkward.
  • Owners who do ask often send a different message each time, or forget to ask at all when the day gets busy.
  • Rough estimate: about 15 minutes to get your link and write two messages you can reuse for months.

Time figures marked "rough estimate" are our estimates, not survey data.

The AI-assisted workflow

Step by step

  1. On a computer, go to your Business Profile and select Read reviews, then Get more reviews. Select Copy to copy your review link. Google says the review QR code can only be made in a computer browser, so right-click it and save it now if you want one for the counter or receipts.
  2. Decide when you'll ask: at checkout, right after pickup, or the day after a job. Google suggests receipts, thank-you emails, the end of a chat and a printed QR code in your store.
  3. Paste the prompt below into your AI assistant, fill in the brackets and ask for a text and an email.
  4. Edit the drafts so they sound like you, then save them as a text shortcut and an email template.
  5. Send the same ask to every customer, not only the ones you think were happy, and offer nothing in return.
  6. When reviews come in, reply to them. Our page on answering Google reviews has a prompt for that.

Copy-paste prompt

I run a [type of business] in [town]. Write two short messages asking a customer for an honest Google review.
Message 1: a text under 40 words.
Message 2: an email under 90 words with a subject line.
When I send it: [right after the visit / the day after the job].
My review link: [paste link]
How I talk: [3 words].

Rules:
- Ask for an honest review, not a 5-star one, and don't suggest what to write.
- No discounts, gifts, entries or anything in return.
- Mention one real thing about the visit only if I add it here: [optional detail].
- Don't invent anything else about the customer or the visit.
- Make it easy to say no.

Works in any AI chat assistant. Replace everything in [brackets].

Optional tool

No new tool needed. Google Business Profile gives you the review link and QR code for free, and any free AI chat assistant can draft the messages.

Watch-outs

  • No incentives. Google's policy says offering free or discounted goods or services in exchange for reviews, or for changing or removing a negative review, is strictly prohibited, and such reviews are removed.
  • Don't ask only happy customers. Google does not allow businesses to selectively solicit positive reviews or to discourage negative ones. FTC staff say asking only customers you think are happy could violate the FTC Act.
  • Don't pressure people to review while they're still at your counter, and don't tell staff to collect a set number of reviews or reviews that name them. Google's policy lists both as not allowed.
  • Don't suggest what customers should write. Google says merchants should not request that specific content be included.

When not to use AI

  • Writing the review for the customer, or posting reviews yourself. That's a fake review, whoever types it.
  • Messaging people who never bought from you. Reviews on Google must reflect a genuine experience.

Quick answers

Where do I find my Google review link?

On a computer, open your Business Profile and select Read reviews, then Get more reviews, then Copy. The same window has the review QR code, which Google says can only be made in a computer browser.

Can I give a discount for a Google review?

No. Google's policy bans offering incentives such as discounts or free goods or services in exchange for reviews. Ask every customer for an honest review instead.
