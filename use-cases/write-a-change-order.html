Write a change order for extra work, signed first

Short answer: Write down exactly what the customer asked for, set your own price and any extra days, and paste both into an AI assistant to draft a one-page change order: scope, what's not included, price change, new total and schedule effect. Check every line, get it signed, and start the extra work only after it is signed.

Trades owners whose customers ask for extra work mid-job

Turn a customer's 'can you also...' into a one-page change order with scope, price and schedule, signed before extra work starts. You set the price.

Trades & ServicesSaves: most of the writing on each change order (rough estimate)

Who it's for

Contractors, remodelers, plumbers, electricians, HVAC, painting and landscaping owners whose customers ask for extra work after the job is agreed.

The situation today

  • The customer asks for one more thing while you're on site. You say yes, do the work, and the extra shows up for the first time on the final invoice.
  • Without anything in writing, the conversation about what was asked for and what it costs happens after the work is done, when it's hardest to have.
  • Rough estimate: 15 to 30 minutes to write a change order from scratch, which is why many get skipped on small extras.

Time figures marked "rough estimate" are our estimates, not survey data.

The AI-assisted workflow

Step by step

  1. As soon as the customer asks for extra work, write down exactly what they asked for, in their words, with the date and how they asked (text, call or on site). Screenshot a text request.
  2. Decide the price (or credit) and any extra days yourself. AI should never set them.
  3. Paste the request, your price, your days and your payment terms into the prompt below. Leave out the customer's name, address and phone.
  4. Check the draft line by line: scope, what's not included, price, new total, schedule and payment terms. Add your customer details and license number in your own form or software.
  5. Send it for signature (e-signature, a signed PDF or paper on site) and start the extra work only after it's signed.
  6. Save the signed copy with the job file and add the change to your final invoice so nothing is a surprise.

Copy-paste prompt

You help me write change orders for my [trade] business. Use only the facts I give you. Do not add work, prices, dates or terms I haven't listed.

Original job: [one line, e.g. 'replace kitchen faucet and shutoff valves']
Original contract price: $[amount]
What the customer asked for, in their words: [paste the text or your notes]
Date they asked: [date]
My price for the extra work: $[amount] (or a credit: -$[amount])
Extra days, if any: [number or 'none']
When payment for this change is due: [terms]

Write a one-page change order with:
1) Change order number [#] and today's date.
2) The scope of the change in plain English, 2 to 4 sentences, plus what is not included.
3) A price table: original price, this change, new total.
4) The effect on the schedule and on payments.
5) This line: 'Work on this change starts only after we both sign.'
6) Signature and date lines for the customer and for me.
If anything is missing or unclear, list it under 'Questions for me' instead of guessing. Keep it under 250 words. Don't add legal language I didn't give you.

Works in any AI chat assistant. Replace everything in [brackets].

Example output (illustrative, not real data)

Change Order #2, Example Plumbing Co. Original job: replace kitchen faucet and shutoff valves.

Scope: Install the customer-supplied garbage disposal under the kitchen sink and connect it to the existing wall switch. Not included: new wiring or a new switch.

ItemAmount
Original contract$[original price]
This change$[your price]
New total$[new total]

Schedule: no extra days. Payment: due with the final invoice.

Work on this change starts only after we both sign.

Watch-outs

  • Never let AI set the price, quantities or extra days. It can sound sure and be wrong. You decide every number; AI only writes it up.
  • Some states set rules for written changes. California's contractor licensing board, for example, says all changes to a home improvement contract, or change orders, need to be in writing and signed by both parties. Check your own state's rules with your attorney or licensing board.
  • Keep the customer's name, address, phone and payment details out of the AI chat; add them in your own form or software.

When not to use AI

  • Big or disputed changes, commercial work with contract terms on change orders, or anything heading toward a claim. Use your normal paperwork and your attorney.
  • Changes you haven't priced yet. AI can't measure the job or know your costs.

Quick answers

What should a change order include?

At minimum: what the extra work is in plain words, what's not included, the price added or credited, the new total, any effect on the schedule and payments, and signature and date lines for both of you.

Can I start extra work before the change order is signed?

This page's method is to get the signature first. California's contractor licensing board says change orders need to be in writing and signed by both parties; rules vary by state, so check with your attorney or licensing board.
