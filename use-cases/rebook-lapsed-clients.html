Win back clients who haven't rebooked

Short answer: List clients who are well past their usual rebooking interval, note their usual service, and have AI draft a few short message styles. Personalize each yourself and offer a specific open time with an easy way to book or say no. Skip default discounts, send in small batches, and text only clients who agreed to hear from you.

about 30 minutes (estimate)

Find regulars who are past their usual rebooking window and send a short, personal note, drafted with AI, that brings them back without discount-dumping.

Personal careSaves: time writing notes; the real win is clients who come back (rough estimate)

Who it's for

Stylists, barbers, nail and lash techs, estheticians, massage therapists and trainers whose business depends on clients coming back every few weeks.

The situation today

  • A regular who came every six weeks hasn't been in for three months. Nobody noticed until they were gone.
  • When owners do reach out, it's a mass discount blast that trains clients to wait for coupons.
  • Rough estimate: a 'who haven't I seen?' check takes 10 minutes in a booking app; writing personal notes is the part that gets skipped.

Time figures marked "rough estimate" are our estimates, not survey data.

The AI-assisted workflow

Step by step

  1. In your booking app or calendar, list clients whose last visit is well past their usual interval (for example, 10+ weeks for a 6-week client).
  2. Write one line about each from memory or your notes: their usual service and one non-sensitive personal detail (a trip they mentioned, their kid's sports season). Keep it out of the AI if it's private.
  3. Run the prompt below for three message styles: a simple check-in, a 'your usual time is open' note and a seasonal one.
  4. Personalize each message yourself and send it from your business phone or booking app. Send a few at a time so you can handle replies.
  5. Ask the ones who don't come back one gentle question: 'Anything we could do better?' and listen.

Copy-paste prompt

I'm a [stylist/barber/esthetician]. Write three short text messages to regular clients I haven't seen in a while.
About my business: [name, services, booking link]
Style 1: a friendly check-in with no offer. Under 35 words.
Style 2: 'your usual [service] time is open', with two specific open slots: [days/times]. Under 40 words.
Style 3: a seasonal reason to come in: [season/event]. Under 40 words.
Use [NAME] and [USUAL SERVICE] as placeholders.
Rules: warm, not needy. No guilt ('we miss you so much!'). No discount unless I list one: [offer or 'none']. Always end with an easy way to book or to say 'no thanks'.

Works in any AI chat assistant. Replace everything in [brackets].

Example output (illustrative, not real data)

Style 2: Hi Maya, it's Jess at Example Salon. Your usual cut-and-gloss spot is open next Tuesday at 10 or Thursday at 4. Want one? Book here: [link], or just reply with a time.

Watch-outs

  • Marketing texts need consent. The FCC says commercial texts require written consent, and people can opt out at any time. Only text clients who agreed to hear from you, and honor 'stop' immediately.
  • Never paste private client details (health, family situations, why they get a service) into an AI assistant. Use placeholders and add the personal touch yourself.
  • Don't send to everyone at once. Batches of a few let you reply quickly when people want to book.

When not to use AI

  • Clients who left after a complaint. Reach out personally, by phone, if at all. A template will make it worse.

Quick answers

How do I get old clients to rebook?

Find clients past their usual rebooking window, send a short personal note with a specific open time and an easy way to book or say no, and skip the guilt.

Should I offer a discount to win back clients?

Not by default. A personal check-in with a specific open slot often works without training clients to wait for coupons. Save offers for a specific reason.

What should I text a client I haven't seen in a while?

Keep it short and personal: use their name, mention their usual service, offer a specific open time and give an easy way to book or say no. Only text clients who agreed to hear from you.
