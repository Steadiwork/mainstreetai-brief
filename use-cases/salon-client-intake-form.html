Salon client intake form: what to ask (with template)

Short answer: Keep it to 8 to 12 questions you will actually use: contact details, the look they want, past services and products, any past reaction to a product, and a checkbox for your cancellation policy. Use AI to draft and trim the questions, then load them into your booking app's form. Ask clients to share health concerns privately with their stylist.

Updated Nov 11, 2026 · By the Main Street AI Brief team · Facts checked against the sources below.

What a salon client intake form should ask, and how to draft one with AI in about 20 minutes. Free prompt, example form and privacy watch-outs. Who it's for: Salon owners, barbers, nail and lash techs, estheticians, spa and brow bar owners who want new clients to arrive with the right details already shared. Saves: the same five questions at every chair (rough estimate)

The situation today

  • Most first appointments start with the same questions at the chair: what look do you want, what have you had done before, what products do you use.
  • Some answers matter for safety. FDA tells consumers to do a patch test every time before dyeing their hair, and says a reaction can happen even if they've dyed their hair before without a problem.
  • Rough estimate: drafting, trimming and loading a short form takes about 20 minutes, once.

Time figures marked "rough estimate" are our estimates, not survey data.

The AI-assisted workflow

Step by step

  1. Write down what you truly need before a first visit: the look they want, past services, products they use at home, and any past reaction to a product you use (hair color, lash adhesive, wax).
  2. Copy your cancellation or deposit policy so clients can acknowledge it on the form.
  3. Paste your list and policy into the prompt below. Don't paste any real client's answers.
  4. Cut every question you won't act on. Ask the AI to mark optional questions and keep the form under about a dozen.
  5. Load the questions into your booking app's intake or consultation form, or print it. Fill it out yourself once to catch confusing wording.
  6. Decide who can see the answers and how long you keep them, and add one plain sentence about that to the form.

Copy-paste prompt

I run a [hair salon / barbershop / nail or lash studio / spa / brow bar]. Help me write a short new-client intake form for [service, e.g. "first color appointment"]. Use only what I tell you. Don't invent policies, prices, products or rules, and don't add medical questions I didn't ask for. If something is missing, write [CHECK] instead of guessing.
What I need to know before the first appointment: [list, e.g. hair history, past color, products used, the look they want]
Product reactions I need to ask about, if any: [e.g. past reaction to hair color or lash adhesive: yes/no]
Our policies to acknowledge: [paste your cancellation or deposit policy]
Where the form will live: [booking app / paper / website form]

Write: 1) a two-line welcome at the top, 2) 8 to 12 questions, each with the answer type (short text, yes/no, multiple choice), grouped under Contact, Your visit today, History and Policies, 3) one line inviting the client to tell their [stylist/tech] privately about anything that could affect the service, 4) a short note on how we use and store their answers. Plain English, friendly, under 250 words, no jargon. Mark each question that isn't truly needed as OPTIONAL.

Works in any AI chat assistant. Replace everything in [brackets].

Optional tool

No new tool needed. Draft with any free AI chat assistant, then use the intake or consultation form in the booking app you already have, or paper.

Watch-outs

  • Keep client answers out of AI chats. Paste only your list of questions, never names, phone numbers, photos or anything about a client's health.
  • Ask only what changes the service. A yes/no question about past product reactions plus a line inviting the client to tell you privately is often enough; check with your attorney or state board before collecting detailed health information.
  • If you color hair, a form doesn't replace the patch test. FDA's checklist says to do a skin test every time before dyeing and to check the ingredient list for anything that caused a past problem, such as PPD.
  • If you tint brows or lashes, know that FDA says no color additives are approved for dyeing eyebrows or eyelashes. Check your state board's rules before you list the service on a form.
  • Read every question the AI writes. It can add a policy, a product or a medical question you never asked for.

When not to use AI

  • Medical spa or clinical treatments that need a medical history. Use forms your medical director or attorney approves, not an AI draft.
  • Waivers and consent language. Have your attorney or insurer provide that wording.

Quick answers

What questions should a salon client intake form ask?

Contact details, how they heard about you, the look they want, past services and home products, any past reaction to a product you use, and a checkbox for your cancellation policy. Keep it to questions you will act on.

Should a salon intake form ask about health?

Ask only what affects the service, such as a past reaction to hair color or lash adhesive, and invite clients to share anything else privately with their stylist or tech.
