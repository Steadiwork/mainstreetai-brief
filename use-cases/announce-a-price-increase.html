Tell customers about a price increase without losing them

Short answer: Decide the new prices and the start date first, giving regular customers several weeks' notice. Write two or three honest reasons, then have AI draft a short notice and a phone script. Tell your best customers personally before the notice goes out, check contracts for fixed prices, and delete any reason or number AI adds.

Updated Oct 18, 2026 · By the Main Street AI Brief team · This page cites no outside facts or statistics.

Use AI to draft a short, honest price-change notice and a phone script for regulars, so customers hear it from you early, with reasons and a date.

Trades & ServicesSaves: most of the drafting time (rough estimate)

Who it's for

Service businesses with repeat or contract customers: lawn care, cleaning, pest control, HVAC maintenance plans, pool service. The same approach works for salons and shops.

The situation today

  • Materials, fuel and insurance went up months ago, but the prices didn't, because the conversation is uncomfortable.
  • When the increase finally happens, it shows up on an invoice with no warning, and the customers you most want to keep are the ones who feel blindsided.
  • Rough estimate: an hour or more of drafting and re-drafting one letter, plus the dread.

Time figures marked "rough estimate" are our estimates, not survey data.

The AI-assisted workflow

Step by step

  1. Decide the facts first: new prices, the effective date (give regular customers several weeks' notice), and whether anyone keeps their old rate (for example, prepaid plans).
  2. Write two or three honest reasons in your own words. Don't make up percentages or blame anyone.
  3. Run the prompt below to get a short notice and a 30-second phone script.
  4. Call or tell your best customers in person before the notice goes out. Send the notice to everyone else.
  5. Keep a short note of every question you get; answer them in a reply template so everyone hears the same thing.

Copy-paste prompt

I run a [type of business]. Write a short price-change notice for existing customers.
Facts (use exactly):
- Service and new price: [old price -> new price]
- Effective date: [date]
- Who keeps the old price and until when: [or 'no one']
- My honest reasons: [2-3 reasons in my words]
- How to reach me: [phone/email]

Write:
1) A notice under 150 words: thank them first, state the change and date plainly, give the reasons briefly, say what stays the same, and invite questions.
2) A 30-second phone script for my longest-standing customers.
Rules: no apologies that sound like guilt, no made-up statistics or percentages, no 'due to inflation' cliché unless I wrote it. Don't promise anything I didn't list.

Works in any AI chat assistant. Replace everything in [brackets].

Example output (illustrative, not real data)

Notice: Thank you for trusting Example Lawn Co. with your yard. Starting March 1, weekly mowing will be $55 (now $48). Our fuel, equipment and insurance costs have gone up over the past two years, and we haven't changed prices since 2024. Your crew, your day and the quality of work stay the same. Customers on a prepaid season plan keep their current rate through the end of that plan. Questions? Call me at [number]. Thank you, Robin

Watch-outs

  • Check your contracts and service agreements first. If a written agreement fixes the price for a term, the increase may have to wait; ask your attorney if you're not sure.
  • Don't paste your customer list into the AI. Write the notice once and merge names in your own email or invoicing tool.
  • Every reason in the notice must be true. If AI adds a reason or a number you didn't give it, delete it.

When not to use AI

  • Deciding how much to raise prices. That's a numbers decision (your costs, margins, local market); work it out with your accountant or your own numbers, not a chatbot's guess.

Quick answers

How much notice should I give customers before a price increase?

Give regular customers several weeks' notice and tell your best customers personally first. Check any contracts for notice terms.

How do I explain a price increase to customers?

Thank them, state the new price and date plainly, give two or three honest reasons, say what stays the same, and invite questions.

How do I tell clients my rates are going up?

Tell your longest-standing clients personally first, then send a short written notice with the new rate, the date it starts, two or three honest reasons and what stays the same. Check engagement letters or contracts for notice terms first.
